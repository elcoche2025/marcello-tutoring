Watch-for

Typing speed. This is the first session where he types every target sentence. Four sentences is the cap; if typing is slow, two locked sentences (hook + focus statement) is a full win. Don't rescue with the mic.

Focus statement = territory. If sentence 2 already tells the examples, sentences 3–4 have nowhere to go. Hold him to the MUST / NOT-YET labels. The pinned plan sentence is the target; his wording can differ, the parts can't.

Reading insertions. Sept 2 he added "on your face." Note any added words in the Clara Barton passage.

The essay is about a real person. If he picks Grandma or Dad and the example gets long and story-like (his strength), that's fine in the PLAN. In the write tab, one sentence of evidence.

Session Plan (~50 min)

Before he sits down: open the app on the iPad, wait for the cloud load, tap 🔄 Reset, make one tap and confirm Cloud saved ☁️. Tap a 🔊 once. Do not open a plain second tab on the computer — use ?view=1.
6 min

Phase 1 — Tab 1: Reading 📖 (Clara Barton)

⚓ Standing foundational-reading rep. Word-map courage (cour = /ker/), soldiers (di = /j/), evidence (-dence). Then the 70-word Clara Barton passage aloud like a storyteller. Timer optional; log WCPM / errors / prosody. Sept 2: ~162 WCPM.

The bridge to the essay: "Where does it say great heart without meaning a real heart?" That's the class's literal-vs-figurative question (RI.4.4). One sentence of talk, then move.

6 min

Phase 2 — Tab 2: Fix-It 🔧

Part 1 — his four verbs. "These are YOUR sentences from the car story. Four verbs are rule-breakers." He says "Yesterday I ___" out loud BEFORE tapping. His ear already knows thought / drove / fought / woke; the point is to make the ear and the pencil agree.

Part 2 — the squish. "A contraction is two words squished into one. A letter gets pushed out, and the apostrophe is the little flag that says a letter used to be here." He taps the letter that falls out of we are · it is · they are · do not. Wrong tap → "say both words, then the squished word — which letter did you stop hearing?"

Six minutes total. This is warm-up, not the lesson — the verbs come back inside his own writing (the radar).

8 min

Phase 3 — Tab 3: Paint the Essay 🎨

"Your class writes essays with four paragraphs, and every paragraph has a color and a job." Read the Clara Barton model together (he reads, or you alternate). For each paragraph: "what is this paragraph FOR?" → he paints it. Wrong color → read the first sentence again.

Then the key move: find the focus statement in the red paragraph — the sentence with the big idea AND both reasons. "That's the boss sentence. Everything else works for it. Yours will look just like it."

If his class has used the Painted Essay already, ask him to teach YOU the colors first. If not, this is his head start.

7 min

Phase 4 — Tab 4: Plan 🗺️

"Your class question is how does someone show a great heart? Who do YOU know with a great heart?" Let him talk first (Grandma from the car story is a natural pick). Then: WHO → reason 1 → a real example → reason 2 → a real example. Chips for the reasons; the mic is fine for the examples.

The app assembles the focus statement from his plan and pins it. Read it aloud together. "In the next tab you type it yourself."

Two different reasons is the gate. If both reasons are really the same thing ("helps people" / "is nice"), push: "what's a DIFFERENT way?"

18 min

Phase 5 — Tab 5: Write ✍️ (red + yellow, TYPED)

Four sentences, each with MUST / NOT-YET labels and a "Type it" tag. Say it out loud, then type. No mic.

  1. Hook (red) — three starters to choose from, or his own. Cheap on purpose: gets him typing.
  2. Focus statement (red) — typed from the pinned plan sentence. THE sentence of the day. Check: person + BOTH reasons.
  3. Topic sentence (yellow) — "First, …" reason 1 only.
  4. Evidence (yellow) — one real time it happened, past tense. This is where the rule-breaker verbs will show up.

🔧 Past-tense radar (NEW): after the finger-point, the app scans for -ed on an irregular verb (thinked, drived, taked, …) and shows the fix; the sentence can't lock until it's gone. Let him say "Yesterday I ___" and fix it himself.

🔍 Sound-alike check runs after that on his TYPED words — the first time it measures him, not the mic. If are/our or to/too shows up, that's the June 8 thread landing.

Cap = 4; win = 2. Hook + focus statement locked is a complete day. Whatever is locked shows in "My essay so far," and next session picks up at sentence 3 or the blue paragraph.

5 min

Phase 6 — Tab 6: Stars & a Wish ⭐

Stars, one wish, tutor notes (blank on purpose — type what he said). Read "My essay so far" aloud together as the send-off: "That's the start of a real essay, and you typed every word."

Parents (~1 min): "We started his essay — the same four-paragraph shape his class uses. Two paragraphs today, two next time. Did a new newsletter come this week?"
