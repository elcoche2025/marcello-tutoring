Marcello's Tutoring Session

Wednesday, May 6, 2026

Session Theme

The Crow & the Pitcher 🐦
Read · Draw · Question · Write

Primary Focus

After CAPE Day 1, when Marcello is tired. Three tabs — read+draw, one big question with Y/N/M scaffold, write 2 sentences anchored to his own drawings. The drawings ARE the source-fidelity scaffold.

The May 4 finding (the reason for this redesign)

"He answered both EBSRs correctly with text + choices visible. But during retell he confidently said the OPPOSITE of the source — and when checked, found a matching word and confirmed."

Marcello shows a Weak Central Coherence + comprehension monitoring pattern (Happé & Frith 2006): strong local processing, weak global gist, self-checking firing at the lexical level instead of the sentence/meaning level. He found a word he had said somewhere in the passage and confirmed "yep, see, it's there" — without checking what the sentence actually said.

Today's design directly targets this: drawings externalize the gist while reading; panel anchors force source-checking at the sentence/meaning level instead of the word level. Story-map drawing scaffolds for HFA are evidence-based (Stringfield, Luscre & Gast 2011; Ukrainetz 1998 stickwriting).

May 4 retell — what he said vs the text

  • "Grandma wanted Tessa to stay in the tide pool" (text says the opposite — Grandma called her back)
  • "Tomorrow Tessa will remember she should stay in the reef" (text: she'll remember to come back when called)
  • When asked "is that what happened? look at the story" — found a matching word, not the matching sentence.
Pattern: lexical match passes for source-check. Local features dominate. Global gist absent.

What's different in today's app

1. 3 tabs, not 5 NEW Read+Draw → Question → Write. Plan tab is gone — the panels are the plan.
2. Drawing canvas per paragraph NEW 220×160 touch canvas. Stick figures + 1-2 word label. The drawings save with the session and carry to the writing tab as panel chips.
3. Y/N/M answer rating NEW For each choice: Yes / No / Maybe. Eliminate the Nos → re-read for the Maybes → pick the surviving Yes. Mirrors the paper process Mekoce found that worked May 4.
4. Panel anchor + source-fidelity check NEW Each sentence anchors to a specific panel before he can write. After writing: "look at the panel, look at your sentence, do they match?" Sentence only locks when sourceCheck === 'match'.
5. Mic + tutor reset (carried over) webkitSpeechRecognition mic button on the textarea + tutor reset button. Same as May 4.

Watch-for

Don't let him bypass the Y/N/M ratings — the scaffold is the work. If he rushes a rating or skips eliminating, slow him down.

If he says "yep that matches" on the source-fidelity check without looking, gently: "let me read your sentence aloud, then read the panel label aloud — same thing?" The verbal mirror often catches what the visual doesn't.

Session Plan (50 min)

3 min

Phase 1 — Greeting + frame today

He's tired from CAPE Day 1. Brief check-in: "How was the test? You doing OK?" Don't grill, don't quiz, don't ask what was on it.

Then frame today: "Today's a short story, lots of drawing. We're going to make a comic strip of it together, then write 2 sentences."

Keep it light. He's done a lot of reading already today. The drawing framing should land as a relief, not more work.

20 min

Phase 2 — Tab 1: Read & Draw the Crow & Pitcher 🐦

One paragraph at a time. Four paragraphs total. ~5 min per paragraph.

Step A — Read. He reads, you read, whichever feels right (he's tired). If he's flagging, you read aloud and he tracks. Tap "I read it".

Step B — Draw the panel. 220×160 canvas. Stick figures, doesn't have to be art. Encourage speed: "just enough so you'd remember what happened in this part." Add a 1-2 word label below.

Step C — Say it in your own words. Tap "I said it in my own words". This is the active paraphrase pause — the retell happens DURING reading, externalized into a visible artifact, before any word-trap can form.

If a panel comes out matching the wrong gist (e.g., he draws the crow flying away from the pitcher when the paragraph is about staying and dropping pebbles), don't correct it directly. Ask: "show me the part of the paragraph this panel is about." Let the gap surface in his own re-reading.

The 4 paragraphs (rough): (1) Crow has been flying, hot, thirsty, sees pitcher. (2) Pitcher has water but the crow's beak can't reach. (3) Crow sees pebbles, drops one in, water rises a little — keeps going. (4) Water rises high enough, crow drinks, flies away with a happy heart.

10 min

Phase 3 — Tab 2: The Big Question with Y/N/M 🤔

One EBSR pair, not two. Central message question (RL.3.2). Walk through the Y/N/M scaffold explicitly — narrate what each rating means before he taps anything.

The Y/N/M script: "Yes if you think this could be it. No if you're sure it's wrong. Maybe if you're not sure."

Part A — "What is the central message of 'The Crow and the Pitcher'?"

  • A. Crows are smart birds.
  • B. Hard problems can be solved step by step. ✓ correct
  • C. Drinking water is important on hot days. (word-trap — "water" is in the passage)
  • D. It is dangerous to fly far from home. (word-trap — "fly" is in the passage)

Part B — "Which sentence from the story BEST shows the central message?" (unlocks on A correct)

  • A. "He had been flying for a long, long time." (distance, not message)
  • B. "His beak could not reach it." (the problem, not the message)
  • C. "With each pebble, the water rose a little higher." ✓ correct (shows step-by-step)
  • D. "He flew away with a happy heart." (the result, not the message)

Don't rush him to rate. Talk through each choice. The wrong answers C and D on Part A are designed as lexical traps — exactly the failure mode from May 4. The Y/N/M scaffold is the defense. If he wants to rate "C — Yes" because "water" is in the story, that's the moment: "is the story about water being important on hot days, or about how the crow figured out a hard problem?"

12 min

Phase 4 — Tab 3: Write 2 Sentences anchored to panels ✍️

Per sentence (~6 min each):

  1. Pick a panel. Tap a panel chip — anchor locks. He cannot write until a panel is anchored.
  2. Look at the panel. Read the label aloud. Say what's happening in the panel.
  3. Write the sentence about THAT panel. Type or use the mic button. Should be about THE PANEL, not the whole story.
  4. Source-fidelity check. The check appears: "Look at Panel X. Look at your sentence. Do they match?" Two buttons: ✓ Yes — they match / ✗ Not yet — let me look again.
  5. Lock. Sentence only locks when sourceCheck === 'match'.

Key script: "Pick a panel. Look at the panel. Now write a sentence about THAT panel. Then look at your sentence and your panel — do they match?"

If he says "yes" reflexively to the source-check: read both aloud together as the slowdown. "OK — let me read your sentence: [reads it]. Now read your panel label: [he reads it]. Same thing?" The verbal mirror catches what the visual doesn't.

The check is at the panel level (global → global comparison) — exactly mirroring EBSR Part B (find the evidence sentence) but for his own writing. This is the mechanism that defends against word-level self-checking.

3 min

Phase 5 — Stars ⭐

Tap stars on sentences he's proud of. No "Wish" today — he just took a test, did a story, and wrote sentences that matched. Today is celebration only.

The pep banner reads:

"You did it. You read the story, drew it, answered the big question, and your sentences match what actually happened. That's exactly the muscle CAPE writing needs."

CAPE Days 2 and 3 are still ahead (Thu + Fri). The framing matters — he's tired tonight, but tomorrow he tests again. The "exactly the muscle CAPE writing needs" line is the bridge.

2 min

Phase 6 — Post-session reflection (Mekoce alone) 📝

Capture in session_notes.txt. The questions are below in the Post-Session Capture box.

Post-session: capture in session_notes.txt

  • Did the canvas drawing actually externalize the gist? Did his panels match what the paragraphs said, or did some panels show the wrong event? If wrong — which paragraph, and what did he draw instead?
  • Did the Y/N/M scaffold help him slow down before picking? Or did he rush through to a final answer? Did he correctly No-out the lexical traps (C and D on Part A), or did he Yes/Maybe them?
  • Did the panel anchor + source-fidelity check actually catch any moment where his sentence didn't match the panel? If yes — what was the mismatch?
  • When the source-check button asked "do they match?" — did he reflexively say yes, or did he actually compare?
  • If he said yes too quickly, what worked to slow him down? Did the read-aloud + compare verbal mirror catch the gap? What other moves worked?
  • How tired was he? Did the drawing framing land as relief, or as more work? Anything to adjust before Thu/Fri sessions?
  • Did he stay anchored to a single panel per sentence, or did sentences drift to "the whole story"?
  • Compared to May 4: any moments where he would have lexical-matched on May 4 but didn't today because the panel was sitting right there?
