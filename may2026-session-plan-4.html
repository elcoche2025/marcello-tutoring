Session Plan (50 min)

3 min

Phase 1 — Greeting + frame

Normal energy. Brief check-in: "How was the rest of last week? All the big tests are done now, right?" Quick, not a quiz.

Then frame today: "Today's a short story — about something a family passes down across generations. We're going to make a comic strip out of it, four panels. Then one big question. Then two sentences."

Connection: "Your class is starting two new books this week — Grandfather's Journey and The Keeping Quilt. Both about families and the things they carry across the world. Today's story is the same shape, just shorter — warming up the muscle you'll use all week."

No Phase 0 recovery today — testing wrapped last Tuesday.

20 min

Phase 2 — Tab 1: Read & Draw "The Little Wooden Bird" 🐦

One paragraph at a time. Four paragraphs total. ~5 min per paragraph.

Step A — Read. Pick early who reads (you or him) and stick with it. If you read, he tracks. Tap "I read it".

Step B — Draw the panel. 320×220 canvas (bigger than May 11). Stick figures, doesn't have to be art. Encourage speed: "just enough so you'd remember what happened in this part." Add a 1-2 word label below.

Step C — Say it in your own words. Tap "Said in own words". This is the active paraphrase pause — retell happens DURING reading, externalized into a visible artifact.

If "unfamiliar" trips him on paragraph 2, word-map it quickly: show the whole word, point at un- (familiar prefix), then familiar. Don't drill — one quick pass.

If a panel matches the wrong gist, don't correct directly. Ask: "show me the part of the paragraph this panel is about." Let the gap surface in his own re-reading.

The 4 paragraphs (rough):
  1. Long ago in a village far away, great-grandfather carved a small wooden bird from his apple tree. He gave it to grandmother on her 7th birthday.
  2. Grandmother as a young woman left the old country, sailed across the sea. The new land was unfamiliar. She held the bird in her pocket the whole way. It helped her feel brave.
  3. Grandmother grew up in the new country, married, had mother. On mother's 7th birthday, grandmother gave her the bird. "Keep it safe," she said. "It remembers our family."
  4. Last week, on the narrator's 7th birthday, mother put the bird in their hand. It is older than the narrator. Older than mother. "It carries a piece of every grown-up I love."
10 min

Phase 3 — Tab 2: The Big Question with Y/N/M 🤔

One EBSR pair. Central message question (RL.3.2). Walk through Y/N/M explicitly — narrate what each rating means before he taps.

The Y/N/M script: "Yes if you think this could be it. No if you're sure it's wrong. Maybe if you're not sure."

Part A — "What is the central message of 'The Little Wooden Bird'?"

  • A. Birds make good birthday gifts. (word-trap — "birthday" appears)
  • B. Family treasures carry love across generations. ✓ correct
  • C. Apple trees grow good wood. (word-trap — "apple tree" appears)
  • D. Brave people cross the sea. (word-trap — "brave" + "sea" appear)

Part B — "Which sentence from the story BEST shows the central message?" (unlocks on A correct)

  • A. "He made it from a piece of his apple tree." (about how, not message)
  • B. "The trip was long, and the new land was unfamiliar." (about voyage difficulty)
  • C. "It carries a piece of every grown-up I love." ✓ correct (shows the message)
  • D. "She married, and she had my mother." (about timeline)

Watch the "birthday" trap. The story OPENS with a birthday and CLOSES with a birthday. He might Yes-rate A because "birthday" feels central. Slow down: "is the story about birthday gifts being a good idea, or about how a small thing from family carries love across many people?"

12 min

Phase 4 — Tab 3: Write 2 Sentences anchored to panels ✍️

Per sentence (~6 min each):

  1. Pick a panel. Tap a panel chip — anchor locks. He cannot write until a panel is anchored.
  2. Look at the panel. Read the label aloud. Say what's happening in the panel.
  3. Write the sentence about THAT panel. Type or use the mic button (now actually transcribes on iPad Safari — interimResults=true fix). Should be about THE PANEL, not the whole story.
  4. Source-fidelity check. The check appears: "Look at Panel X. Look at your sentence. Do they match?" Two buttons: ✓ Yes — they match / ✗ Not yet — let me look again.
  5. Lock. Sentence only locks when sourceCheck === 'match'.

Key script: "Pick a panel. Look at the panel. Now write a sentence about THAT panel. Then look at your sentence and your panel — do they match?"

If he says "yes" reflexively: verbal mirror. "Let me read your sentence: [reads it]. Now read your panel label: [he reads it]. Same thing?"

If sentence 1 takes > 7 min, end the write phase at one sentence. One locked sentence with a real source-check > two rushed ones. This is the clean-code re-test — quality of mechanism > volume.

3 min

Phase 5 — Stars + dialogue seed ⭐

Tap stars on sentences he's proud of. No "Wish" today.

The pep banner reads:

"You did it. You read the story, drew it, answered the big question, and your sentences match what actually happened. That's the same muscle your class is using on Grandfather's Journey and The Keeping Quilt this week."

Then plant the dialogue seed: "Did you notice the grandmother SAYS something in the story? 'Keep it safe.' That's called dialogue. Your class is going to do more of that this week." Don't dwell — just seed it.

2 min
