Watch-for

Summer slide: he did NOT get the summer app, so assume no structured practice. If WCPM drops or first-letter guessing is back, that's the baseline — log it, don't react to it. It usually recovers in 2–3 weeks.

Sound-alikes: the ear can't catch these, so don't ask him to "listen." Ask "does this one mean a PLACE, or 'we are'?" If he sails through the Word Detective but still swaps one in his own sentence, that's the real data point (recognition ≠ recall).

Proofread auto-correct: if he taps a word and says the intended word instead of what's written: "read me ONLY this word — sound it out." Or read his sentence back verbatim so he hears the gap.

Territory collapse: if sentence 1 already says how he felt, sentence 3 has nowhere to go. Hold him to the MUST / NOT-YET labels.

Session Plan (~50 min)

Before he sits down (5 min earlier): open the app on the iPad, wait for the cloud load, tap 🔄 Reset (top-left) to clear any test state, make one tap and confirm Cloud saved ☁️. Tap a 🔊 button once so Safari unlocks speech. Test the mic on one textarea.
6 min

Phase 1 — Tab 1: Welcome Back 👋

Open warm and unhurried — and happy birthday (Aug 31) first. Then: "You're a 4th grader now! Before we do anything, I want to hear about your summer." Let him TALK first — a real oral narrative, the way he does best. Then capture the short version in the app (chips + a few typed/dictated words). This interview IS the story plan for Tab 4.

Callback that will land: in June he wrote about going to Portugal to get a new car. "Did it happen? Tell me about the car." (There's a "The new car 🚗" topic chip if it did.)

Q4 — "What's 4th grade like? What are you learning?" — is your only class intel so far. Listen for the teacher's name, a book title, a math topic.

End the tab by picking ONE story topic (chip or his own words). Gate: where + best day + topic.

8 min

Phase 2 — Tab 2: Reading Warm-Up + fall baseline 📖

⚓ Standing foundational-reading rep. RF.4.3/4.4 continues last year's documented weak spot. Today it doubles as the fall baseline.

Word-mapping: through (ough = /oo/ — he missed this on Feb 23 AND Mar 9 last year; make a moment of it if he nails it), excited (ci = /s/), journey (jour = /jer/). Tap "Find the tricky part" → he reads the WHOLE word.

Timed read (baseline): tap the ⏱ 1:00 timer, he reads the welcome passage (65 words) aloud "like a storyteller." Count errors. Enter WCPM, errors, prosody /4. If he finishes before the minute, have him restart from the top and keep counting — or just note "finished in __ sec."

June 8 was 148 WCPM / prosody 3. A drop is normal after summer. First-letter guessing back? Note it — that's the pattern to track over September.

5 min

Phase 3 — Tab 3: Word Detective 🔍

Frame it as a mystery, not a lesson: "Some words are sneaky — they SOUND exactly the same, so your ear can't catch them. Only a detective can — by asking what the word MEANS."

Four rule cards (tap to hear): are/our · where/we're · there/their/they're · your/you're. Read each swap-test out loud together: "we are"? → we're. Belongs to us? → our. A place? → where/there.

Six cases. Case 1 is his own June sentence, fixed: "___ getting a new car." Before he taps, he says the swap-test out loud. Wrong tap → hint + try again (no penalty, the app just counts tries).

This is the receptive warm-up. The real test is Tab 5, where the same check runs on HIS sentence. Don't over-teach here — 6 cases, then move.

8 min

Phase 4 — Tab 4: Times As Many ✖️ (quiz Thursday)

Eureka Math² M1 Topic A — multiplicative comparison. The picture is the whole concept: draw ONE bar for the small number, then that same bar again and again. "4 times as many as 3" = the 3-bar, four times. The app draws the tape diagram for each problem.

Say the sentence every time before tapping: "___ is ___ times as many as ___." Then the equation: ___ × ___ = ___. This is the language the quiz uses.

Five problems: two "how many?" (unknown total), one "how many TIMES?" (unknown multiplier — the one kids miss), one "___ times as many as 7" (no story), one match-the-sentence for 24 = 4 × 6. Wrong tap → "count the bars" hint, no penalty.

If he confuses "times as many" with "more than" (additive), draw it on paper: 3 + 4 = one bar plus a little; 3 × 4 = the bar repeated. Note which he defaults to.

18 min

Phase 5 — Tab 5: My Summer Story ✍️

Draw it first (the topic from Tab 1 is pinned above the canvas), 1–2 word label. Then three sentences, each with a territory label:

  1. Sentence 1 — what happened (who + where + what). NOT YET how he felt. Say it → write → source-check vs drawing → finger-point → sound-alike check → lock.
  2. Sentence 2 — dialogue (3rd rep). Tie-in: his class is doing "punctuation with quotations" this week (L.4.2b) — "you're ahead of them on this." Who is talking → exact words → tell-word → "Put it in the box" → SAY it out loud in a talking voice — "could a real person say that?" → checks → lock.
  3. Sentence 3 — feeling + why. Feeling chips (greyed once used) + connecting-word chips (because / so / when / although). Must tell something NEW. Checks → lock.

🔍 Sound-alike check (NEW — the thing to watch): it only appears after the finger-point proofread. If his sentence has are / our / where / we're / there / their / they're / your / you're / to / too / two / its / it's, it asks "You wrote ___. Which one did you MEAN?" Let him answer from meaning, not from you. If he picks a meaning that doesn't match the written word, the app says which word he needs — he fixes it in the box, and the check reruns. If a sentence has no sound-alikes, it passes automatically (say so — "nothing sneaky in this one").

👆 Finger-point: tap-and-read each word alone. The auto-correct habit is the thing to watch: intended word instead of written word → "read me ONLY this one."

Two good sentences beat three rushed ones. If time is short, skip sentence 3 and keep the checks intact on 1 and 2. Lock what's done.

5 min
