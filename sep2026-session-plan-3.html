Finish the Essay 🎨

Wednesday, September 16, 2026

Session Theme

An essay is a paragraph that grew
blue + green today · then read the whole thing

Primary Goal

Finish his first 4th-grade essay ("My mom has a great heart"): the blue proof-2 paragraph and the green conclusion, typed, then read the whole thing aloud. Around that, answer the Sept 9 finding head-on: holding the essay's structure and each paragraph's structure at the same time is hard. The fix is one idea, taught concretely — an essay is a paragraph that grew — plus a skeleton that stays on screen so he never holds both at once. Research brief: 2026.09.16_research_what_is_an_essay.md.

The order that works (protect it)

SAY the whole essay in one breath → open ONE color → say the sentence → TYPE it → finger-point → verb check → twin check → lock. No mic on target sentences.

Chant (four beats, four colors): "Big idea. Prove one. Prove two. Say it again." Say it together at the Zoom Lens and again before each paragraph. Watch for him reaching for it unprompted.

What's new (from the research + Sept 9 data)

1. Zoom Lens NEW · Hochman: paragraph → MPOThree moves: (a) say his essay in ONE BREATH (four sentences, pinned); (b) match the four parts of a small paragraph to the four parts of an essay — same job, bigger size; (c) the chant. Only then does he write.
2. The -ed rule NEW · "helpt"-ed sounds three ways (/t/ jumped, /d/ played, /id/ wanted) but is ALWAYS spelled -ed. Fix-It part 2, plus the verb radar now catches helpt / jumpt / playd / wantid.
3. Twin check NEW · cared/carried…The four Sept 9 substitutions (cared→carried, wounded→wooden, evidence→events, floods→fludes) are the reading words, a Fix-It "which word did you MEAN?" set, and new twin pairs inside the write-tab check.
4. Scramble, in-session 2nd repA fresh paragraph ("Grandpa listens" — the exact shape of his blue paragraph) and a fresh essay ("My Uncle Has a Great Heart"), built into Tab 2. Standalone pages still on the site for extra reps.

Watch-for

The prior essay. The app loads last week's red + yellow from the cloud on the iPad at startup. If the load misses, tap 🔴 or 🟡 in the skeleton and type them in from your notes (fields are there). Sentence 5 (proof 2 topic sentence) he already wrote last week — it lived outside the four slots, so paste or retype it into sentence 5 and let him check it rather than rewrite it.

One-breath essay. If he can't say the four sentences, don't write yet. Say it together twice, then he says it alone. This is the structure check, separate from spelling.

Conclusion = restate, not repeat. "Same idea, different words." If he copies the focus statement word for word, that is fine as a first pass; then "now say it a new way" for ONE word at a time.

Typing pace. Four sentences is the cap; blue alone (sentences 5–6) is a full win. Green can carry to a short day 3 with the read-aloud.

Session Plan (~50 min)

Before he sits down: open the app on the iPad, wait for BOTH cloud loads ("Loaded from cloud" / "Last week's essay loaded"), tap 🔄 Reset, then open ✍️ Write and tap 🔴 and 🟡 to confirm his red and yellow paragraphs are there. Paste sentence 5 (last week's proof-2 topic sentence) into the blue box. Tap a 🔊 once.
6 min

Phase 1 — Tab 1: Tricky Words 📖

⚓ Standing reading rep, but built from HIS data: cared, wounded, evidence, floods — the four words that came out as look-alikes last week. Each card names the twin to beat. Say it slowly → tricky part → "what does it MEAN?" Then a short Red Cross passage with all four inside; timer optional.

If he reads "carried" for "cared" in the passage, stop on it: "one syllable or two?" The twin is a reading habit and a spelling habit at once.

7 min

Phase 2 — Tab 2: Scramble 🧩

Paragraph first: "Grandpa listens" — five strips. When it's solved, say it out loud: "reason 2, then a real time it happened. That is exactly your blue paragraph today."

Then the essay: "My Uncle Has a Great Heart." When solved, read the GREEN paragraph together: "See how it says the focus statement again in new words, then why it matters? That's your green today."

Drag by pressing and holding, or ▲▼. Hints target the mistake. Keep it to 7 minutes; the standalone pages are there for another day.

6 min

Phase 3 — Tab 3: Fix-It 🔧

Part 1 — the one that got away: "fought" (he chose "fighted" last week) plus "thought" as a warm-up. "Yesterday I ___" out loud first.

Part 2 — the -ed rule: "Last week you wrote help-t. That IS how it sounds. But the past-tense ending has three sounds and ONE spelling." Tap /t/ jumped, /d/ played, /id/ wanted. Four items: helped, jumped, played, wanted. "Your ear hears three; your pencil writes one."

Part 3 — which word did you MEAN? cared/carried, wounded/wounded, evidence/events in sentences. Meaning decides.

7 min

Phase 4 — Tab 4: Zoom Lens 🔍 (what IS an essay?)

The line to say: "Here's the secret. An essay is a paragraph that grew. Your paragraph has a topic sentence, details, and a closing. An essay has the SAME shape — but every sentence grew into a whole paragraph. Same shape, two sizes."

  1. One breath. His four-sentence essay is pinned (built from his plan). He says it out loud, twice if needed, then alone. "If you can SAY that, you already have the whole essay."
  2. Match the shapes. Small paragraph on the left, essay on the right. Tap a sentence, tap the essay part with the SAME JOB. Wrong tap → "what is that sentence FOR?"
  3. The chant. "Big idea. Prove one. Prove two. Say it again." Four beats, four colors. Say it together.

Keep one metaphor per level: the class's Painted Essay colors for the essay; the sandwich (Feb) can stay for a paragraph if he brings it up. Do not add a house or a hamburger.

18 min

Phase 5 — Tab 5: Write (blue + green, TYPED) ✍️

The four-color skeleton stays on screen; red and yellow show ✓ (tap to read them); ONE color is open at a time. That is the working-memory fix: he never holds both structures, the screen holds one.

  1. Sentence 5 — proof 2 topic sentence (blue). Last week's, pasted in; he checks it, finger-points it, locks it.
  2. Sentence 6 — the evidence (blue). "A real time she listened when you were sad." Past tense: -ed and the rule-breakers.
  3. Sentence 7 — say the focus again, in new words (green). Pinned focus statement is right there. "Same idea, different words. Both reasons."
  4. Sentence 8 — why it matters (green). The last line of the whole essay. "What does it mean to you that she has a great heart?"

🔧 The verb radar now catches helpt / jumpt / playd / wantid as well as fighted. 🔍 The twin check now includes cared/carried, wounded/wooden, evidence/events. Let him fix, don't fix for him.

Cap 4, win 2. Blue locked = a full day. Green can spill into a short day 3.

6 min

Phase 6 — Tab 6: My Essay ⭐

The whole essay, four colors, on one screen. He reads it aloud, start to finish. Then stars, one wish, tutor notes. Send-off: "Four paragraphs. Big idea, prove one, prove two, say it again. You wrote a whole 4th-grade essay."

Parents: "He finished his essay today — I'll send it in the note. Next week we start the second one with less scaffolding. Any newsletter this week?"
