Primary Goal

It's the last session of the year. Two jobs: (1) send him off feeling proud — make the growth visible and concrete; (2) hand the parents a real summer plan they're asking for. Everything is a primitive he already owns — this is a low-pressure, high-warmth session that confirms two things one more time (the Dialogue Builder, and the new finger-point proofread) on a happy, self-chosen prompt.

The order that works (protect it)

DRAW → SAY it out loud (act it out) → THEN write. His cleanest sentences all year came this way; cold writing tangles. Today's summer story rides on the SAME order. Don't let the say-aloud step get skipped — especially on the dialogue sentence.

A future progress marker is Marcello reaching for the say-aloud step himself. Note it in session_notes if he does.

Two things we're confirming

1. Finger-point proofread NEW · §14 fix The Jun 1 "reads what he MEANT, not what's on the page" finding. After a sentence is written + source-checked, the app shows the words as chips; he taps each one in order and reads ONLY that word aloud. Forces his eyes onto the real graphemes. FIRST real test — watch whether he catches a mismatch himself.
2. Dialogue Builder 2nd rep · W.3.3.b Jun 1 split: receptive worked, expressive (writing his own) was hard — he put narration inside the quotes. The lever was act it out / say it aloud. Same builder, happy summer prompt, ONE more rep. Lean on say-aloud; expect it to still be hard — that's normal 3rd grade.

Watch-for

Proofread auto-correct (the headline thing): when he reads his own writing he reads the sentence he MEANT. The finger-point tool fights this, but he may still "auto-read" the right word. If he taps a word and says the intended word, gently: "read me ONLY this word — sound it out." Or read it back to him verbatim (errors and all) so he hears the gap.

Dialogue: watch for narration inside the quotes (what HAPPENED vs what someone SAYS). Lever = read the quoted words aloud in a talking voice: "could a person actually SAY that out loud?"

This is a celebration — keep it light. If a sentence is hard, one is plenty. The win today is that he leaves proud, not that he produces volume.

Session Plan (~50 min)

10 min

Phase 1 — Tab 1: Look How Far You Came 🎉

Open by naming it: "This is our LAST one before summer. Before anything else, let's look at everything you learned this year."

Go through the milestone cards together. Tap each one he remembers. Read the little cheer line. This is the emotional core — slow down and let him feel it. Tie each to a memory ("remember the egg-drop essay? the Statue of Liberty story? the Red Scarf?").

Then the "which are you PROUDEST of?" chips. Whatever he picks is right. Ask him WHY — a sentence of reflection is gold.

Growth that's true and worth saying out loud: prosody 2.5 → 3.0; "When/Although" sentences now grammatical; first narratives; dialogue receptively solid. He has come a real distance.

8 min

Phase 2 — Tab 2: Summer Reading Warm-Up 📖

⚓ Standing foundational-reading rep (~5 min — don't skip even today). RF.3.3/3.4 is his documented regression. Keep the rep on the last day too.

Word-mapping: three summer words — vacation (-tion=/shun/), adventure (-ture=/cher/), beautiful (beau-=/byoo/). Tap "Find the tricky part" → highlight + hear it → he reads the WHOLE word. Fights the first-letter-guessing habit.

Prosody pass: he reads the short summer passage aloud "like a storyteller." Log WCPM / prosody in the tutor fields if you take them. This is the foundational-reading rep, done in a fun summer wrapper.

If he first-letter-guesses one of the words: whole word → point at the tricky chunk → reread. One pass, no drill.

22 min

Phase 3 — Tab 3: My Summer Story (dialogue + finger-point) ✍️

His own prompt: one fun thing he wants to do this summer. Draw it first (320×220 canvas), add a 1–2 word label.

Sentence 1 — narrative (warm-up, known skill). Say it aloud → write → source-check against his drawing → finger-point proofread → lock.

Sentence 2 — DIALOGUE (the 2nd rep). Make someone TALK about his summer.

  1. Who is talking? → I / My friend / My mom / My dad
  2. What did they say? → the exact words
  3. Tell-word → said / asked / answered
  4. Preview assembles it → "Put it in the box."
  5. SAY the whole sentence out loud (act it out) — the lever.
  6. Source-check (matches drawing? quotes hug ONLY spoken words?) → finger-point proofread → lock.

👆 Finger-point proofread (NEW — the thing to watch): the app shows each word as a glowing chip. He taps left-to-right and reads ONLY the touched word aloud, slowly. If he says the word he MEANT instead of the word on the page, that's the auto-correct pattern — stop and have him sound out just that one word. Whether he notices a mismatch on his own is the key data point for session_notes.

📣 Dialogue self-instruction chant (in app, tap to hear): "Who's talking? · What did they say? · Hug the words." Say it OUT LOUD together before the build. Watch for him reaching for it unprompted.

Keep it light. If the dialogue sentence is a grind, one good sentence is a complete success today. Don't let the last session feel like a test.

7 min

Phase 4 — Tab 4: My Summer Plan + Goodbye ☀️

Show the Summer Adventure pack on screen (the 4 preview cards) so he's excited to use it. Then he picks one reading goal + one writing goal (chips or his own words).

Hand the parents the pack: the printed marcello_summer_packet.pdf + the web link. Walk them through it in ~2 min (see tutor docx for the parent script): 20 min reading/day with the reading log; the two home routines (dialogue check + finger-point read); the writing-prompt menu. Reassure: low-tech, no pressure, 10–15 min a day.

Final stars + the send-off banner. Let him hit "Finish — my summer starts now!" for the double confetti. End warm: "I'm proud of you. Have an amazing summer."
